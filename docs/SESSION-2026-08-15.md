## What was actually wrong, and why it survived

Five defects sat underneath almost everything else. Each had passing tests over it.

### 1. The prologue did not play for a player who obeyed the invitation
`bell?.Arm()` had two production call sites, and the live one sat inside `GmThreshold`'s `lockNow`,
which required the player to pass the gate **and then move backward**. A player who did what the
letter said — walk to the house — reached the porch, read one line, and stood there forever. No
tolls, no crossing, no Entry Hall. The entire nine-count sat behind an optional glance over the
shoulder. *(Nick's call: arm on crossing the gate house-ward. `e512f05`.)*

### 2. The estate house had no collision at all
Not its doors — the building. `addColliders: 0` on the FBX, nothing added one, and `BuildManor`
actively disabled any collider it found in the footprint. The player walked through the front wall
and out the back while `GmThreshold` printed "The doors did not open" from route projection.
Threshold Refusal is the whole opening. *(`d7ddfdf`, fixed in `e512f05`, proven in `2f4693b`.)*

### 3. Gates 3–12 were structurally unreachable, not merely un-run
Gate 2 ran the entire EditMode suite and broke on first failure; Court's tests failed *by design*
behind a content lock. So the opening could not be verified until Court was fixed, and Court could
not be touched until after the walk that verification was meant to precede. A circular dependency,
not a scheduling gap — which is why it survived multiple sessions. *(`d17d169`.)*

### 4. A fresh clone was broken
`npm run test:fast` — the command CLAUDE.md tells you to run after every change — invoked four
scripts, **none of which existed in git**. Neither did `sync-unity-scenes.mjs`, the tool the entire
"edit the repo copy, never the synced copy" model depends on. 16 load-bearing scripts were
untracked. *(`23de0cb`.)*

### 5. Six finished rooms had no player in them
Court's trial, the Parlor's cheating host AI, Shut the Box's rules engine, the Hidden Room's shard
mechanic, the Labyrinth's chase — all real, all unit-tested, none reachable. **A room nobody can walk
into is not 0% built; it is 100% built and 0% reachable, and a phase bar cannot tell those apart.**
*(`539947a`.)*

## The through-line

Every one of the above was invisible to a green test suite, and for the same reason: **the tests
measured whether the player could REACH things, in a game whose thesis is being STOPPED.** That
sentence came from the review panel and it explains the collision bug, the gate bug and the bell bug
as one failure rather than three.

So a large part of the day went into the instruments rather than the game.

- **Gate 0, `scripts/test-harness-integrity.mjs`** — feeds every verification script input it must
  reject *and* input it must accept, failing if either direction is wrong. Proven by sabotage: with
  a guard deliberately re-blinded, the isolated case still passed and only the *combined* case
  caught it, which is why each guard needs a paired case. *(`96707bc`.)*
- **Conditions travel with the number** — `performance.json` records cores/RAM/GPU/batch-mode and
  `unity-cli` prints them beside every p95, including inside the failure message:
  `p95=19.25ms [load 23.03 · 2.30/core ⚠loaded]`. *(`96707bc`.)*
- **A self-expiring failure allowlist** — Court's exclusion carried its reason and its retirement
  condition, and reported *itself* stale on the first run after Court was fixed, refusing to go green
  until deleted. *(`d17d169`, evicted in `4a1db0c`.)*
- **A UI-only exit for the composition contract** that announces itself on every run and *fails* a
  scene that declares it then authors real geometry. *(`5f778e8`.)*
- **Test runs own their verdict** — `unity-cli` used to kill a test run on any `[Gm*] FAILED` in the
  log, which reported a *passing* test's own evidence as the failure. *(`c18fad5`.)*

## Defects found and fixed

**In the opening:** the bell arming; the house collision; a canon violation (the first line inside
the house said *"The doors give without a sound"*, two minutes after the porch refusal); beats
running at 640–730 wpm against a hardcoded 4.2s dwell; the nine-count's payoff line destroyed by a
same-frame call ordering; and `GmSymptoms` putting a lowpass on the AudioListener that progressively
muffled the very bell the ending counts.

**In the rooms:** five props standing off the floor they were declared to stand on (a mirror
floating 1.06m, a gate buried 1.90m, a bookshelf and door floating over a metre); Court's 15
findings (a 2.1m desk inflated to **12.6m wide** by an inherited non-uniform scale, a chair 4.4m wide
standing *inside* the north wall, a `Cube()` helper assigning world position after parenting);
**and the dice in the dice game rendering as 6.9:1 wafers.**

**In the harness:** an existing-but-empty directory passing the render-defect gate clean; gate 12
hardcoding a scene path the registry owns; `GmSceneDirector` never clearing its static on destroy
(subtle: Unity's `==` reports a destroyed object as null, C#'s `?.` does not).

## Two things worth carrying forward

**When a fix breaks a test, decide which one describes the intent before touching either.** Gate 3
caught a regression from this session's own toll-nine fix within minutes of first being reachable —
and the test was wrong. It had encoded "toll nine IS the crossing," which was a description of the
bug. The reflex to make the test pass again would have deleted the fix and restored text no player
could read.

**Measure before naming a cause.** Gate 8's failure looked exactly like the 216,820 triangles of
mansion collision added hours earlier. The history said it had been failing worse for two weeks. The
obvious suspect was innocent, and only the measurement showed it.
