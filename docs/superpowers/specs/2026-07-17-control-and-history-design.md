## The question, and the questions under it

"Who is actually in control?" decomposes into: What is the house? What did the first keeper do?
What are the sevens? What does the stag mean? Why THIS shape of guest? Does anyone sit above
Aldric? The design goal is not one answer — it's an **answer architecture**: every ending and
artifact confirms a *facet*; no two facets contradict; no scene adjudicates. That's what makes a
fandom argue for years instead of reading a wiki page once.

## Three candidate architectures

**A. THE LEDGER (recommended).** The house is the standing body of an unpayable debt. Sometime
before the inn (L5), the first keeper — ruined by a wager — dug his cellar deeper than any cellar
needs to be and came back up with an *arrangement*: the table stays open, seven games a night,
and the house never wants. What he owes, and to whom, is the hole in every record (deliberately).
Control answer: **nobody living. A contract is in control.** Aldric is a debtor-in-service; every
host is. "FOR THE HOUSE, FROM ITS WINNERS" — the monument was raised by hosts, generation after
generation, each one a winner serving out the same instrument.
*Why it's right for THIS game:* it retro-justifies the true ending mechanically — catching 8+
cheats isn't beating Aldric, it's **documenting fraud against the contract**. A rigged game voids
the instrument. "Finally." It also mirrors the protagonist's own arc (a man drowning in debt walks
into the house that IS one) and the coach-house chalk (41. 9. — the house keeps meeting the same
shape of desperation, because that's who says yes; not fate, selection bias).

**B. THE UNDERSIDE (flavor layer, not the operative answer).** Something is under the hill —
older than the road, which is why the road *wends*: it bends around what the first surveyors
wouldn't cross. Whether the contract's counterparty is a mind, a hunger, or nothing at all — a
self-enforcing instrument with no one left holding the other end — is the game's deepest
unresolved question, and it stays unresolved. The Labyrinth (the manor's "hidden underside") and
the chapel-door scratches "from under the door" belong to this layer.
*Use:* imagery and dread, never mechanics. The forum war this preserves: "is there a creditor,
or is the debt itself alive?" Both readings survive every ending. That is the point.

**C. Psychological recursion (rejected).** The house as manifestation of the guest's own debt.
Rejected: the bible's "not destiny" rule kills it, and it cheapens six materially-real endings.

**Recommendation: A operative + B atmospheric.** One sentence for the team: *the house is a debt
that outlived its parties; hosts are its collection agents; the true ending is a fraud case.*

## The deep history (L5 → L0, one story through six wrappers)

- **L5 · The Turn (pre-1700s):** The road bends at the hill for no reason maps explain. Drovers'
  superstition: don't stop at the turn after dark. The first keeper builds exactly there.
- **L4 · The Inn (1700s):** Prosperous beyond sense. The keeper "keeps the table" — seven games
  nightly in the taproom, house never loses more than it can afford, and the inn never wants.
  Tally marks begin (the pier plaque's sevens: nights kept, grouped by the week).
- **L3 · The Club (early 1800s):** Gentlemen formalize the table into a private club; the manor
  face goes up. The crest is minted: **a stag with one antler snapped** — commemorating, the club
  said, a coach-stag that balked at the gate, turned against the traces, and snapped its antler
  on the iron rather than walk the drive. They made the refusal a trophy. (The crest is a warning
  wearing heraldry: the only creature on record that turned back. The secret ending's player IS
  the stag; "the road only turns one way" — and the stag turned anyway. Never stated anywhere.)
- **L2 · The Failed Manor (mid-late 1800s):** A keeper's family tries to stop keeping the table.
  The house does not punish; it *waits*. The family loses everything by ordinary means — bank,
  blight, a child lost to fever during a counting game the household never spoke of (the small
  white marker: COUNTED OUT). The manor "fails." The lesson every later host learns from the
  records: the house doesn't need to enforce the contract. The world does it for them.
- **L1 · The Invitation House (Aldric's era → now):** A desperate guest wins the table and the
  chair transfers — the bible's mechanism. Aldric has kept it "centuries" by the house's clock
  (the Labyrinth and the house's interior time are not the road's time; left vague). Invitations
  go out in his hand, to whoever is drowning: the same mason's hand on century-apart headstones
  is the same phenomenon as the same handwriting on century-apart letters — the house keeps its
  servants' hands.
- **L0 · Tonight:** The narrator. 41 and 9 and a number he won't say. The house has met him
  before — not HIM; his *shape*. That's what the chalk in the coach house is.

## The sevens (one rule, many sightings)

Seven games a night is the TERM of the keeping — the original arrangement's only visible clause.
Everything else radiates: seven games in the invitation, tallies grouped in sevens, the menu
kicker, (proposal) the Labyrinth's 7×7, the bell that tolls for 7 seconds. Never explained; the
number simply won't stop being seven. Fans will build numerology; all of it will be "confirmed."

## Who's in control — as experienced, ending by ending

No ending names the counterparty. Each confirms a facet:
- **Escape:** the house lets you go *because the contract has no clause about walking out mid-
  night* — and Aldric watches from the window (the figure the player maybe saw on the drive in).
- **Replacement:** the chair transfers on a win — the contract executing, nobody deciding.
- **True ("Finally"):** fraud voids the instrument. If a counterparty existed, it never appears
  to object — the scariest confirmation of B's "maybe nobody's left" reading.
- **Hollow:** the house spends a guest to no purpose. Contracts don't do mercy; neither can he.
- **Secret (never sat down):** costs the house nothing. Indiscriminate instruments don't grieve.
- **The friend in the walls** stays exactly as the bible has it: prior host, ambient, unresolved.
