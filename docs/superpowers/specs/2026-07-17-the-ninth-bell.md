# The Ninth Bell — Prologue Ending Canon

> **Supersedes the KO half of `2026-07-14-opening-threshold.md`.** Threshold Refusal's core survives
> intact: the front doors never open, you are not admitted as a caller, you wake inside having never
> earned the civil doorway. What changes is **what takes you**. Not a hand at the collar. The hour.
>
> Owner decision, 2026-07-17. Companion to `2026-07-12-story-bible-final.md` (Aldric, endings) and
> `2026-07-14-house-history.md` (site, the old road).

---

## The decision

The invitation gives a time: **nine o'clock**. The chapel bell tolls the hour. On the ninth toll the
player is taken — wherever they are on the grounds, whatever they were doing. They come to inside the
house as a clock finishes its own ninth chime.

The bell and the clock are the same nine. The count did not stop; it changed rooms.

## Why nine (this is not arbitrary)

Nine is already the house's number, everywhere, and nothing had ever explained why:

- **Nine names** in the Entry Hall ledger. The ninth worn illegible from touching.
- **Nine tiles** in Shut the Box, mapped in ledger order to those nine guests.
- **Nine portraits** on Court's jury wall — the same nine, watching you stand where they stood.
- **COUNTED OUT**, the two-word epitaph on the child's marker.

The bell tolling nine is the house **counting**. Not marking a time — taking a tally. Every guest who
ever answered the letter was counted in at nine o'clock, and the ledger is the receipt.

This costs nothing to establish and retro-loads every nine already in the game.

## Why the bell beats the hand

| | Hand at the collar | The ninth bell |
|---|---|---|
| Who takes you | a person, implied but never seen | the house itself |
| Where | the porch only | anywhere on the grounds |
| Player agency | must funnel to the porch to trigger the ending | explore freely; the hour comes regardless |
| Tone | mugging | appointment |
| Fits "the house uses people" | a thug does the using | the house needs no thug |

The threshold spec's own words are *"the house's side of the bargain."* A bell is the purest reading
of that. A house that runs on ledgers, portraits and tallies does not send someone to hit you. It
expects you at nine.

It also removes a real design failure: the old ending required the player to walk to the porch. Now
the grounds are explorable on their own terms and the ending is inevitable rather than triggered.

## The payoff line (already planted, three weeks ago)

Beat 2, z=38, shipped and untouched:

> *"Forty-one thousand, to men who don't take excuses. Nine thousand more to the bank. **There was a
> version of me that paid on time — Mara married that one.** Now there's a stack of envelopes by the
> door I stopped opening in March."*

A man who lost his wife, his money and his daughter's surgery to being unreliable is on time for
exactly one thing in his life. He does not notice. The player does.

The closing beat, at the ninth toll:

> **"The card said nine."**
> *"I have never been on time for anything in my life. Ask Mara. Ask the bank. Ask the men who call
> before sunrise. I was early for this."*

Never explain the compulsion. He explains it himself, badly, which is the character.

## The invitation

Canon (`house-history.md`): the property is "the house on Wend Hill" — **never a street address,
never a family name**. "Wend" is an old word for a turn in a road; the drive that turns you toward
the house is the name. The site's oldest guest-visible layer (L4) is a coaching inn on a road that
mattered once, and coaches ran to timetables.

So the invitation gives directions, not an address, and an hour, not a deadline:

```
        THE HOUSE ON WEND HILL

             Nine o'clock.

     Take the old road. It will turn you.
       Come as you are. Bring nothing.

                              — a friend
```

- **No "do not be late."** The compulsion is never named by the house. Naming it would make it a
  threat; unnamed, it is a fact.
- **"It will turn you"** is the name doing double duty: the road wends, and so does he.
- **"Bring nothing"** reads as courtesy on stage 1 and as inventory on stage 3. You are cargo for the
  ledger night.
- **"— a friend"** is the letter's three-stage reveal endpoint per story bible §6 (Aldric's own hand,
  written to the next guest the way he wishes someone had written to him). It stays "a friend" here.
  Do not confuse with "the friend in the walls" (the host before Aldric, never resolved).

## The nine tolls

Fixed real-time cadence, unaffected by route or action. Inevitability is the point: *no matter what
you do, you are counted in at nine.*

| Toll | Fires | What the player gets |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 45s after the gate locks | Distant. Just a bell. The chapel has a bell after all. |
| 2 | +30s | Closer than it should be, wherever you walked. |
| 3 | +30s | The crickets stop between tolls, then resume a beat late. |
| **4** | +30s | **First symptom.** A spike behind the eyes. Audio lowpass opens. Faint vignette. |
| 5 | +30s | Heartbeat enters, under everything. Lowpass deepens. Slow camera drift. |
| 6 | +30s | A high whine. Chromatic aberration. His own footsteps sound like someone else's. |
| 7 | +30s | Ambience cuts out entirely. Only bell, heart, whine. |
| 8 | +30s | Vision tunnels hard. Walk speed decays. His footsteps go silent. |
| **9** | +30s | Black. |

Total ≈ **4m 45s** of grounds after the gate. Enough to walk every branch; short enough to feel like
a held breath.

**A real bell does not take five minutes to ring nine.** Nobody says this. The unnatural spacing is
the tell, and it is deniable right up until it isn't.

### Rules

- The bell is **diegetic and locatable**: it comes from the chapel (x≈28.9, z≈30). Louder near it,
  quieter far, never quiet enough. Distance attenuates volume; it does not attenuate the count.
- **No escape.** Reaching the porch early does not trigger anything. The doors still do not open
  (Threshold Refusal). You stand there and the hour arrives anyway — which is worse.
- **The secret ending still wins.** Retreating to the car before the gate ends the game before the
  first toll (story bible §7). The bell can only count someone the house already has.
- Symptoms are **monotonic**. They never recover between tolls. Nothing the player does helps.

### Retired

`GmRareEvents`' old chapel-bell rare event — "stand through 3 tolls and it knocks you back" — is
**superseded**. That was a curiosity; the bell is now the clock. Do not ship both.

## The crossing

The single most important seam in the Prologue. Get this wrong and it is a loading screen.

1. **Toll 9.** Black. Not a fade — a cut. Sound cuts with it.
2. **~2s of nothing.** No image, no sound. Long enough to feel like a mistake.
3. **Hearing returns first**, underwater, wrong-ended: reverb tail before its own impact.
4. **The whispers** (below) surface in the dark while there is nothing to look at.
5. **Sight returns as a slow iris**, out of black, badly — chromatic fringing, focus hunting.
6. **The clock.** As vision resolves, a longcase clock somewhere in the room is finishing its chime.
   The player hears **the ninth chime only**. The bell's ninth toll and the clock's ninth chime are
   pitched to cross-fade into one another: same fundamental, different room.
7. He is inside. He never saw a door.

**The reveal is arithmetic, not dialogue.** The bell rang nine. The clock finished nine. No time
passed. He was not carried — he was *expected*, and he arrived on the hour, and some part of him
walked in on its own.

Closing card, on the last chime:

> *"Nine o'clock. On the hour. For the first time in my life, I was not late."*

## The whispers

Heard in the dark between hearing returning and sight returning. Roughly 6–8 seconds.

**Design rule: unintelligible by construction.** These are texture, not lines. Two or three words are
allowed to surface; everything else stays at the edge. A whisper you can parse is a threat. A whisper
you nearly parse is the house.

Words permitted to surface:
- *"...counted..."* — ties the marker, the ledger and the bell in one word.
- *"...nine..."*
- *"...on time..."* — his own rationalization, said back to him by something that is not him.

Everything else is breath, room tone, and the shape of speech without its content. Never a name.
Never "friend" (§6 collision). Never anything a player could quote in a wiki as a fact.

### Voice-over: not needed, and not wanted yet

Because the whispers are unintelligible by design, they are **processed audio, not performance**: a
layered breath bed with two or three near-words. Building this VO-ready is free; committing to VO now
buys casting, direction, retakes and localization for a scene that is deliberately incomprehensible.

**Real VO is a Phase 9 (Steam) decision and it belongs to Aldric**, whose dialogue slips are the
game's actual performance surface (story bible §2: *"…forgive me. Old habit, that last word."*). The
Prologue does not need a voice. If we ever get one, this scene takes it as a drop-in.

## The branches

"Multiple paths, at different points, with different beats." The grounds already have the geometry —
8 walk rects, authored and shipped in the design data, currently parsed and unenforced. What they
lack is **beats of their own**: all 5 existing beats are z-line triggers on the drive, so a player who
turns off the drive walks in narrative silence.

Each branch gets one entry beat, fired once, on entering its rect. They are the same man thinking, so
each one is about the debt, the daughter, or the wife — never about the scenery.

| Branch | Rect | Entry beat |
|---|---|---|
| East side path | `[3, 13.2, 26.3, 30.7]` | The gravel turn toward the graves. |
| Cemetery | `[8.6, 24.6, 12.6, 40.6]` | The open grave, the stag, the surname. |
| Chapel forecourt | `[24.6, 38.5, 26, 34]` | The bell is *right there*. And it is not helping. |
| West side path | `[-13.2, -3, 19.8, 24.2]` | The turn toward the garden. |
| Ruined garden | `[-23.6, -8.6, 15.6, 34.6]` | The scarecrow in a better coat than his. |
| West flank | `[-26, -21, 34, 41]` | The long way round. Delay dressed as curiosity. |
| Coach yard | `[-33.5, -23, 40, 56]` | The chalked numbers: 41. 9. And one scratched out. |

Copy is written in Task N of the plan, not here — this doc owns **why**, not final prose. Voice check
before any of it ships.

**The chapel forecourt beat carries the most weight.** The player can walk to the bell. They can stand
under it. It tolls anyway, in their face, and nothing they do to it matters. That is the thesis of the
whole game in one room, four scenes early.

## Addendum, 2026-08-13 — the count is contextual (Owner decision)

Supersedes **"Fixed real-time cadence, unaffected by route or action"** (§The nine tolls) and the
literal reading of *"no matter what you do, you are counted in at nine"* as timing-independent.
Full mechanism in `2026-08-13-the-reckoning.md`.

**What survives, unchanged:**
- The count is nine, always. The ninth toll takes you wherever you stand.
- The crossing sequence — cut, dead air, whispers, iris, the ninth chime — is untouched.
- Symptoms remain monotonic. Nothing recovers between tolls.
- The only escape remains retreating to the car before the gate locks (the secret ending).

**What changes:** the *spacing* between tolls now reads what happened on the grounds — which
outbuildings were entered, what was found, how long the player lingered — inside a bounded,
config-asserted window (`reckoningMinimumGroundsSeconds`–`reckoningMaximumGroundsSeconds`). The
inevitability claim is not weakened, it's enforced numerically instead of by a constant: the
ninth toll always lands inside that window, for any player behavior. What the player did decides
how long they get first, never whether they're counted in.

**Why this is better, not weaker.** This spec's own argument against the old porch-KO was that
the ending should be inevitable rather than triggered by walking to a specific spot. A cadence
that ignores the player entirely is inevitable, but also inert — a countdown timer wearing a
bell. A cadence that visibly notices what the player did, and still cannot be escaped, is worse
for the player, which is the actual point of the house. The unnatural spacing was always the
tell ("a real bell does not take five minutes to ring nine"); spacing that shifts with what you
were doing is a louder tell, and still just as deniable.

**Ships dark.** `reckoningPressureAuthority` defaults to `0` and jitter defaults to `0`, so the
schedule collapses to exactly `45 + 8×30 = 285s` — bit-for-bit today's shipped cadence — until
Nick turns the dial on a walk. Open question 1 above ("4m45s — right, or too long?") is now a
*range* the player's own actions move inside, not a single number to approve.

The estate's outbuildings becoming genuinely enterable (starting with the coach house) is the
reason this exists — see `2026-08-13-the-reckoning.md` and the implementation plan for scope,
which outbuildings, and what's still explicitly undecided (the chapel in particular).
